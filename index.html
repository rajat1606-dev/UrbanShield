Transparency first. The formula below is a documented heuristic for exploration and teaching. It is not scientifically validated and must never replace official advisories from IMD, NDMA or CWC.

Weighted scoring formula

Score = Σ (normalised factor × weight) ÷ Σ (weights of available factors). If a factor is unavailable its weight is redistributed across the rest — nothing is invented.

Data transparency

OBSERVED live sensor / station reading (Open-Meteo current conditions) MODEL weather-model output mixing observed + forecast hours MEASURED terrain elevation from a public DEM (ASTER 30 m / Copernicus 90 m) OFFICIAL published statistics (Census of India 2011, city level) ESTIMATE analyst tier informed by public records — not a measurement UNAVAILABLE API/dataset unreachable — weight redistributed, nothing fabricated

Drainage-network data and ward-level population are not available through any free API; both use clearly-marked qualitative tiers. Flood history is compiled from public records listed on the Data Sources page. Land-use classes are cross-checked against live OpenStreetMap land-use tags wherever the Overpass service responds.

Scenario sliders

SIMULATED
Rainfall increase+0%

Multiplies the rainfall factor score (0–100) by up to 2× — models cloudburst / monsoon-spike stress.

Drainage improvement+0%

Reduces the drainage-vulnerability factor score — models upgrades to storm-water capacity.

Green cover increase+0%

Reduces the land-use factor score — models permeable surfaces, parks and retention.

Recalculation is instant: every location's weighted score is recomputed with the adjusted factors and re-classified (0–30 Low · 31–60 Medium · 61–100 High).

HEURISTIC · NOT ML

The six-factor weighted score is a transparent heuristic (rainfall exposure, elevation, density, land use, drainage, flood history). The ML model instead learns from labelled flood history. Related questions, different methods — compare them, don't merge them.

Population — Census of India 2011

OFFICIAL

Ward-level population is not published through a machine-readable API, so scoring uses the official city-level density plus a clearly-marked relative density tier per locality (1 = sparse … 5 = extremely dense).

CityPopulationAreaDensity (computed)

Source: censusindia.gov.in — Primary Census Abstract, 2011 (latest released at this granularity). Densities are computed in-app from the published population ÷ area.

Documented historical flood events

PUBLIC RECORDS

Historical hazard portals — Bhuvan (NRSC) and NDEM (NRSC) — publish flood hazard layers as manual portal downloads (WMS/shapefile), not as a key-less JSON API, so they cannot be fetched live from a browser. The per-locality flood-history factor is therefore a documented analyst estimate informed by the events above.

Disclaimers

  • UrbanShield AI is an educational demonstration of open-data integration and transparent multi-factor scoring.
  • The weighted formula is not scientifically validated. Scores are relative indicators, not probabilities of flooding.
  • This tool does not provide official disaster warnings. Follow IMD, NDMA, SDMA and city municipal corporations for advisories.
  • Simulated ("what-if") values are hypothetical and kept separate from all live observations.
  • Locality coordinates are real neighbourhoods shown at approximate points; ward boundaries are not publicly available via API.
  • The ML Flood Prediction section trains real scikit-learn models (Random Forest, Decision Tree, Logistic Regression) on the IMD Kerala rainfall dataset with published flood labels (1901–2018); predictions outside Kerala are experimental extrapolations, and the six-factor scoring engine remains a separate non-ML heuristic.
